# Nepal road-material policy: evidence review

Research date: 18 October 2026. Prepared for the Nepal Open Source workspace. Sources, dates, exact supported facts, locators and limitations are also recorded in [sources-nepal.json](sources-nepal.json). All source claims below are paraphrases except the short Nepali phrase explicitly quoted.

## What the statement establishes

The user-supplied screenshot attributes to Surya Raj Acharya a reasonable methodological demand: promote domestic production, but choose pavement material using technical and economic evidence, and disclose the study. The linked X page could not be retrieved by the web tool. The screenshot is therefore the source for the statement, not independently authenticated X metadata. The pictured OnlineKhabar story was located and matched.

The public evidence supports requiring a transparent comparative appraisal. It does **not** establish that a particular industry captured the decision, that no relevant studies exist, or that one material is universally superior.

## The policy already contains a suitability test

The official **Public Procurement Use and Promotion of Domestic Products Directive, 2083** was posted on the Prime Minister's Office website on 30 Ashoj 2083 / 16 October 2026. The original scanned Nepali PDF was downloaded; pages 2 and 3 were visually inspected because its embedded OCR text is corrupt.

- Section 5(3), PDF page 2, conditions the road-material preference on **“प्राविधिक रूपमा उपयुक्त भएमा”**: if technically appropriate. It names domestic-cement concrete, local stone pavement, and other suitable construction methods.
- Section 5(4) requires a study/design report to explain the technical justification for foreign materials or technology and why domestic alternatives are unsuitable.
- Sections 7 and 8 address availability, quality, technical performance, supply timing and documented reasons for exceptions.
- Section 4 retains quality and technical standards; section 6(1) retains safety and durability requirements.

Interpretation: the most useful response is to make that suitability test measurable and public. A report should compare feasible pavement systems, local supply and delivery, whole-life cost and risk. This is a reading of the reviewed clauses, not an official translation of the complete directive.

Sources: [official landing page](https://opmcm.gov.np/content/606/guidelines-on-use-and-promotion-of-indigenous/), [official PDF](https://giwmscdnone.gov.np/media/pdf_upload/IMG_0002_a7yupar.pdf). Local copy: [directive PDF](sources/2026-domestic-products-procurement-directive.pdf). Visual inspection: [page 2](sources/inspection/directive-2.png), [page 3](sources/inspection/directive-3.png).

The [matching OnlineKhabar report](https://www.onlinekhabar.com/2026/10/2040571/government-to-prioritize-construction-of-cement-roads-in-place-of-blacktopped-roads), dated 16 October 2026, also retains the technical-appropriateness condition in its article text. Its headline alone is an incomplete description.

## Engineering resources already available in Nepal

### Flexible pavement: DoR 2021

The Department of Roads' flexible-pavement guideline considers traffic, wheel loads, climate, terrain, subgrade strength and available material. The main framework covers traffic above 2 million standard axles (msa), with annexes for low-volume roads and surface dressing. It includes cement-treated base/subbase, crack-relief layers and stress-absorbing membrane interlayers.

Implication: “bitumen versus cement” is too narrow as an engineering choice. Cement can strengthen the foundation while asphalt provides a replaceable wearing surface. A surface label alone does not identify the load-bearing system.

Source: [DoR flexible guideline](https://dor.gov.np/qrdc/publication/guidelines-and-manualsguidelines-and-manuals/force/guidelines-for-the-design-of-flexible-pavement-2-21), April 2021. This is design guidance, not an economic appraisal of the 2026 policy.

### Rigid pavement: DoR 2021

The rigid guideline covers jointed plain concrete pavement and treats traffic fatigue, temperature stresses, foundation support, drainage, dowels and joints. Its stated scope is roads with daily commercial traffic above 450 vehicles of more than 3 tonnes laden weight; it explicitly excludes low-volume rural roads. It commonly references 4.5 m transverse joint spacing. Its generally stated 30-year design period is a target, not a warranty. The traffic procedure specifies a continuous 48-hour axle survey and sample fractions by traffic volume.

Implication: concrete is an engineered slab and foundation system. Replacing asphalt with unverified concrete thickness does not implement this standard. Rural concrete sections need an applicable low-volume design basis.

Source: [DoR rigid guideline](https://dor.gov.np/qrdc/publication/guidelines-and-manualsguidelines-and-manuals/force/rigid-pavement-guideline-2-21), 2021, printed pages 10–12 and drainage/joint chapters.

### Cement-treated base: DoR 2024

Nepal has a dedicated cement-treated base/subbase construction guideline. It sets seven-day unconfined compressive strength ranges of 4.5–7 MPa for CTB and 1.5–3 MPa for CTSB. Cement dosage must satisfy the mix-design strength and durability criteria. It also discusses crack-relief layers. A fixed cement percentage is a preliminary cost assumption, not a sufficient acceptance specification.

Implication: a hybrid pavement can use an existing institutional method. It still requires laboratory trials, controlled mixing, compaction, curing, and reflection-crack management.

Source: [DoR CTB/CTSB guideline](https://dor.gov.np/home/publication/guide-to-road-slope-and-protection/force/26-dor-pbl-gdl-2024-026-cement-treated-sub-base-base-guideline--dor-2024-final), June 2024, printed pages 1–3 and 7.

### Existing analytical tool

The [DoR Pavement Analysis System](https://fpd.dor.gov.np/manual) calculates layered elastic responses and fatigue/rutting checks. Its manual labels generic material properties as preliminary and calls for site-specific tests. This review did not run a project structural design. The tool is a resource for the proposed engineer-led pilot, not evidence that the illustrative website sections are structurally verified.

### Historic cement-capacity benchmark, clearly dated

NRB's May 2021 study reports 2018/19 installed capacity of 15 million tonnes/year across 55 cement industries, domestic production of 7.49 million tonnes and demand of 9.05 million tonnes. Its seven-factory operational sample averaged 50.41% capacity utilization. The report discusses certification, inconsistent quality and bulk-supply issues alongside local economic benefits and pollution.

This establishes a historical domestic manufacturing base and underutilization. It must not be represented as Nepal's 2026 capacity. It also shows why nominal factory capacity is not enough to prove ready supply for road contracts. Sampling limits are important: only two private domestic plants were included, and community respondents were selected non-randomly.

Source: [NRB study, May 2021](https://www.nrb.org.np/contents/uploads/2021/05/2078.01.25-Final-Draft-of-Cement.pdf), executive summary items 4–5 and section 1.4.

## Nepal's road problem includes water, slopes and continuity

The World Bank's 2022 Nepal Country Climate and Development Report identifies heavy rainfall, flooding and landslides as major road-network hazards. It estimated direct strategic-road-network climate-damage repair costs at NPR 3–6 billion annually over the following decade, with indirect costs around 50–75% of direct costs when damage is addressed promptly.

This is a model-based projection from 2022, not a measured annual current expenditure. It is inappropriate to attribute the entire amount to asphalt, or to claim concrete would remove it.

Engineering inference: drainage, erosion protection, slope stability, outlet maintenance and flood-safe structures are common prerequisites for every pavement option. A slab can bridge small local support differences but cannot make an unstable slope or a washed-out embankment safe. Geographic and climate exposure should be a decision input before material preference.

Source: [Nepal CCDR](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099415012072225780/pdf/P176737-6645dc69-0e51-4d05-9eb7-4d74af8c17ba.pdf), 2022, section 3.2.

## Does a comparative report exist?

**Some comparison literature and official design guidance exist. A published, nationwide comparative appraisal specifically supporting the October 2026 directive was not located in the sources reviewed.** This is a bounded search result, not proof that no report exists.

- A 2022 Pathlaiya–Nijgadh study compares asphalt pavements with cement-treated and granular bases. For a 15-year design with 34.86 msa traffic, 5% annual growth and 8% effective CBR, the authors report 5.63% lower initial and 24.39% lower combined initial/maintenance estimates for CTB. This is one modeled corridor, not a universal saving or observed service-life experiment. [Original journal PDF](https://www.nepjol.info/index.php/joetp/article/download/49579/36879/146699), DOI: 10.3126/joetp.v3i1.49579.
- A [2022 NRIC review](https://nric.org.np/research/comparative-study-of-flexible-and-rigid-pavement/) explicitly argues for location-specific studies of subgrade, traffic and costs. It is not the policy's national evidence base.
- A [2018 Butwal case-study abstract](https://ijtimes.com/paper-view.php?id=IJT-2021-F19274) describes a 24 km flexible/rigid comparison and reports higher rigid initial cost. Only its abstract was reviewed here; its numerical results have not been adopted as planning assumptions.

Search covered the linked post, matching news report, OPMCM directive, DoR guidelines and design-document listings, official customs/NRB sources, and accessible Nepal research pages. Procurement files, unpublished consultant reports and all provincial/local repositories were not exhaustively searched.

## Decision requirements suggested by this evidence

These are analytical recommendations, not claims made verbatim by a source.

1. **Hard feasibility gates before scores:** hydraulic drainage/outfall, stable earthworks, sufficient subgrade support, traffic category, legal quarry supply, workable traffic diversion, and demonstrable contractor quality control. An infeasible option should not win through domestic-content points.
2. **Compare equivalent service:** same road width, axle spectrum, design reliability, safety, drainage and analysis period. Include repairs, renewals, lane closures and residual value consistently.
3. **Separate economics from industrial policy:** show agency cost and user disruption separately from domestic value added and foreign-exchange exposure. Domestic purchasing is not automatically net foreign-exchange saving because cement can embody imported fuel/equipment.
4. **Use a corridor-specific materials plan:** asphalt where repair access and utilities dominate; properly engineered concrete where repeated heavy/slow loads and stable foundations justify it; cement-treated foundations with asphalt where verified mix/design and crack control offer the best balance.
5. **Make evidence inspectable:** release traffic and axle data, soaked-CBR/geotechnical results, trial-mix results, drain/outfall drawings, priced quantities, maintenance assumptions, sensitivity cases and conflict-of-interest declarations.
6. **Measure outcomes before scaling:** instrument matched pilot sections, including at least two monsoons; measure distress, roughness, joint/edge failure, drain condition, maintenance spending, closures and complaints. Early monitoring is not proof of 30-year life.

## Evidence hygiene for the website

Use “modeled” for financial estimates and “illustrative” for layer sections until site data and professional design exist. Use “not located in sources reviewed” for the missing nationwide report. Label trade statistics FY 2024/25. Never advertise guaranteed 30–40 year concrete life or automatic annual asphalt failure. Do not frame historic cement capacity as current. State that the actual directive already includes technical suitability and documented exceptions.
