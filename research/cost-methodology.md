# Pavement economics and selection methodology

Prepared 18 October 2026. Currency: Nepalese rupees (NPR); NPR10 million =1 crore. The model is a transparent screening exercise, not a bill of quantities for tender, a national policy impact estimate, or a completed pavement design.

## What the calculation can support

The conditional recommendation is **choose pavement by road segment, after fixing drainage and verifying support**. A targeted portfolio has the most merit in the stated mixed-corridor scenario. This does not mean a prescribed70/30 mix is best for Nepal, or that concrete automatically cures a water problem. The model recommends asphalt in ordinary sections and concrete only where its additional capital cost buys a sufficiently valuable service-life advantage.

In the baseline, the targeted portfolio costs about NPR53.56 crore upfront for10km including stated contingency and VAT. Its discounted30-year agency cost is NPR53.98 crore before VAT and contingency. Uniform asphalt needs less upfront cash; uniform concrete concentrates more expenditure at the start. These two figures measure different things and must not be added together.

The recommendation depends materially on **an assumed4-year asphalt resurfacing interval in stressed sections**. If that interval is5 years under the same other assumptions, uniform asphalt is cheaper. Local investigation could therefore reverse the recommendation.

## Evidence versus inputs

| Category | What is established | What is assumed here |
|---|---|---|
| Design | Nepal DoR publishes separate flexible and rigid pavement guidelines | Illustrative layer thicknesses; neither has been structurally designed for a selected road |
| Prices | DoR rate-analysis norms and a current Kathmandu district material/wage schedule exist | All installed composite rates, labour/haul implications, and cost ranges |
| Tax | Nepal IRD's FAQ gives13% VAT | Application to the modeled contract budget; actual tax estimate requires verification |
| Economics | Discounting and consistent remaining-service treatment are established LCCA methods |30-year horizon,5% real discount, service lives and maintenance events |
| Road conditions | Nepal's roads have varied climate, loading, terrain and access needs | A hypothetical accessible10km corridor containing70% ordinary and30% high-stress segments |
| Sustainability | Material origin alone cannot establish lifecycle emissions | No quantified carbon benefit; environmental matrix scores are neutral |

The installed rates are **not copied from a current district schedule or contractor quotation**. A district schedule supplies basic materials, labour and equipment inputs, not a ready universal installed pavement price. Before procurement, build each composite rate with the relevant district's current schedule, DoR norms, actual quarry and plant leads, fuel and binder quotations, verified taxes, and contractor capabilities. Kathmandu rates cannot simply be transported to a remote mountain road.

## Equal comparison boundary

All alternatives provide the same1km ×7m carriageway,7000m² of paved area. Total shoulder width is1.5m outside the carriageway. The10km example contains70,000m² of carriageway.

This is reconstruction on an existing, stable, accessible alignment. Land purchase, bridges, tunnels, large culverts, retaining structures, deep weak-ground treatment, major new earthworks, slope stabilization and utility relocation are excluded. Their costs can dominate in Nepal. Drainage is included only as a concept allowance, not a hydraulic design.

The options are:

- **A: drainage-first flexible asphalt throughout.** Common formation/subbase/drainage plus200mm wet-mix macadam,60mm dense bituminous macadam and40mm asphalt concrete. This is a quantity example, not a recommended thickness.
- **B: jointed plain concrete throughout the candidate corridor.** Common formation/subbase/drainage plus150mm dry lean concrete, separation membrane,250mm plain concrete slab, dowels/ties and designed joints. The150mm DLC example follows DoR2021's major-highway minimum recommendation; the slab and full section still need site-specific design. It is a comparator for a corridor that could be designated suitable for concrete, not a national concrete mandate.
- **C: targeted portfolio.** The baseline allocates7km to A on ordinary stretches and3km to B on stressed stretches. Add NPR1.5 million for transition/interface work over the10km project, plus the same design/supervision percentage. Survey data must determine the actual split.

High stress means a location where recurring slow heavy axle loading, braking or other demonstrated service demands make flexible surfacing renewal relatively frequent. Persistent flooding, scour, landslide movement, unstable fill or uncorrected drainage are **not** solved by assigning concrete. Those are hold points requiring another engineering solution.

## Upfront cash budget

Let D be direct works. Design/supervision =5%D. Base capex =1.05D. Contingency =15% of base capex. VAT cash allowance =13% of base capex plus contingency. Total budget =base×1.15×1.13. VAT on contingency is a budget reserve assuming it is spent on taxable work; a contingency reserve is not itself a taxable supply.

|10km project, NPR crore | A asphalt | B concrete | C targeted |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Direct works |33.56|52.04|39.25|
| Design/supervision |1.68|2.60|1.96|
| Base capex |35.24|54.64|41.22|
|15% contingency |5.29|8.20|6.18|
|13% VAT allowance |5.27|8.17|6.16|
| **Initial cash budget** |**45.79**|**71.01**|**53.56**|

There is no inflation/escalation allowance: all prices are constant NPR at the analysis date. Update the estimate before tender and add project-specific nominal cash-flow escalation separately if needed. The15% contingency is a stated budget choice, not evidence that scope and geological uncertainty fit within15%.

## Maintenance schedules and discounted cost

Each alternative is assumed to meet the same service target throughout the30-year study with the listed maintenance. No field-calibrated deterioration model establishes that assumption.

| Event, per km | Ordinary asphalt | Stressed asphalt | Ordinary concrete | Stressed concrete |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Common drain/shoulder/safety upkeep per year |180,000|180,000|180,000|180,000|
| Surface-specific routine work per year |150,000|300,000|80,000|150,000|
|40mm asphalt renewal |Years8,16,24|Years4,8,12,16,20,24,28|—|—|
| Cost of each asphalt renewal |7,250,000|7,250,000|—|—|
| Joint reseal,260,000 each |—|—|Years5,10,15,20,25|Years5,10,15,20,25|
| Full-depth slab repair at years15 and25 |—|—|3% slab area each|5% slab area each|
| Repair unit rate |—|—|5500/m²|5500/m²|

The7.25 million renewal comprises280m³×22,000=6.16 million,7000m²×120=0.84 million milling/preparation, and0.25 million traffic control. Routine budgets cover inspections, small patching/crack sealing and isolated maintenance outside scheduled major renewal; they must be disaggregated during estimating to prevent duplicated bills. Concrete repair rate includes saw-cutting, removal, localized base restoration and new slab. Resealing rates do not include full slab replacement.

Costs are paid at year end, apart from initial capital at year0. Present value:

`LCC = C0 + Σ[(routine_y + periodic_y)/(1+r)^y] − residual_30/(1+r)^30`

The model assumes maintained foundations and initial structural components serve the30-year horizon. It contains no catastrophic reconstruction, utility reinstatement, earthquake, landslide, major flood or overload-event loss. If inspections imply earlier structural rehabilitation, add it; merely shortening the wearing-course interval does not represent a structural failure.

Residual treatment: shared assets and initial structure are assumed consumed at year30. Localized concrete repairs restore the original service period and receive no assumed extension credit. Last asphalt renewal receives a pro-rata credit for unconsumed resurfacing service, including preparation as part of the renewal package: ordinary asphalt24→32 has2/8 remaining, stressed asphalt28→32 has2/4 remaining. No material recycling/scrap credit is claimed for either type. This convention is explicit but uncertain; longer verified concrete structural life would change the comparison. A zero-residual variant is included in `results.json`.

|30-year agency cost, NPR million per km | Ordinary | Stressed |
|---|---:|---:|
| Asphalt |50.37|66.84|
| Concrete |60.20|61.87|

These figures explain the portfolio choice: under baseline service-life assumptions, concrete is economically favorable only on the stressed segments. C's interface allowance is then added to the weighted selected sections.

|30-year result,10km, NPR crore | A asphalt | B concrete | C targeted |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| **Discounted agency LCC** |**55.31**|**60.70**|**53.98**|
| Difference from A |—|+5.39|−1.33|

LCC excludes contingency, VAT and non-agency impacts. It includes initial design/supervision. It is not a full social cost-benefit analysis: user delay, road-user vehicle operating costs, crash outcomes, emissions, land and fiscal externalities are not monetized. Do not label it an economic internal rate of return or promise taxpayer savings of this amount.

Under baseline cash flows, C's cumulative discounted agency expenditure first falls below A in year20 and remains below to year30. B never crosses A during the study. That is a modeled crossing, not a guaranteed payback; capital spent over a real construction programme and uncertain intervention dates will change it.

## Sensitivity and break-even evidence

For a stressed segment at5% real discount and baseline rates, concrete versus asphalt30-year cost changes as follows:

| Assumed asphalt renewal interval | Concrete minus asphalt, NPR million/km | Lower modeled cost |
|---|---:|---|
|4 years|−4.97|Concrete|
|5 years|+0.76|Asphalt|
|6 years|+4.55|Asphalt|
|8 years|+9.20|Asphalt|

The switch falls between integer intervals4 and5. This is a screening trigger to investigate, not a physical performance threshold. More frequent asphalt renewal could arise from the wrong mix or bad workmanship, which should first be corrected rather than treated as an inherent property of asphalt.

The deterministic grid includes3 real discount rates(3%,5%,8%),3 stressed shares(0%,30%,60%),3 stressed asphalt intervals(4,6,8 years),3 asphalt-specific cost factors(0.8,1,1.2), and3 concrete-specific factors(0.8,1,1.2):243 cases. Unique agency-cost winners are A106 cases, B45, C26;66 cases tie, generally because A and C coincide at zero stressed share. **These are not probabilities**: the grid has arbitrary equal sampling and does not encode Nepal's actual frequency of these conditions. It demonstrates that the baseline winner is not universal.

The frontend JavaScript counterpart reproduces the Python calculations, including remaining service and routine maintenance, so parameter changes are substantive recalculations rather than scaled graphics.834 accounting and parity comparisons passed across the baseline and243 scenarios. Such tests establish arithmetic consistency, not realism of prices or predicted pavement life.

## Selection matrix and interpretation

All scores run from1(poor) to5(strong); higher is preferable. Cost scores are `5×lowest cost/option cost`. Remaining scores and **all** weights are analyst judgments, openly editable. They are not measured Nepal field observations. Environmental evidence is missing, so all options receive the same neutral score.

| Requirement | Weight | A | B | C |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Agency lifecycle cost |20%|4.88|4.45|5.00|
| Initial affordability |10%|5.00|3.22|4.27|
| Fit to varied corridor |20%|3.00|4.00|5.00|
| Deliverability/QA |10%|4.50|2.50|3.50|
| Recurring maintenance management |10%|2.50|4.00|3.50|
| Domestic supply/import exposure |10%|2.00|4.50|3.00|
| Construction access/reopening |10%|4.50|2.00|3.50|
| Utility access/adaptation |5%|5.00|2.00|4.00|
| Environmental evidence |5%|3.00|3.00|3.00|
| **Weighted result/5** |**100%**|**3.83**|**3.56**|**4.13**|

C's fit score presumes successful segment diagnosis, utility coordination, competent construction of two systems and sound transitions. If that capacity is absent, reduce the score. Concrete's domestic-supply score does not assume zero imports: fuel, machinery, some process inputs and steel still matter. Asphalt uses substantial domestic aggregate and labour too.

Some preferences overlap. Affordability is deliberately separate from LCC because a binding yearly budget differs from long-run cost. Maintenance-management reliability is the non-cash risk of carrying out interventions, not a second estimate of their monetary cost. Construction access reflects service inconvenience not monetized in LCC. Reviewers should combine/reweight these if they judge overlap excessive.

Three explicit weighting profiles produce three different rankings:

- Balanced baseline: **C4.13 > A3.83 > B3.56**.
- Budget and access priority: **A4.37 > C4.12 > B3.37**.
- Domestic supply and maintenance priority: **B3.93 > C3.74 > A3.01**.

One-weight-at-a-time sweeps redistribute all other weights proportionally. On the5-point grid, A overtakes C when upfront affordability reaches40%, construction access or QA reaches35%, or utility adaptability reaches30%. B overtakes C when domestic-supply weight reaches35%. All42 single judgment-cell changes of±1 retain C in the baseline matrix, but combined score changes can reverse it. Reducing corridor-fit weight to5% or less reverses the winner to A. This identifies which value judgments carry the recommendation.

## Construction productivity and duration checks

The following are resource assumptions, not Nepal verified production records. They estimate **productive surfacing days**, not elapsed project duration:

- Asphalt production/placement500t per productive day; assumed compacted density2.35t/m³. A's10km requires70,000×0.10×2.35=16,450t, or32.9 productive days for both asphalt courses combined. Base, tack, haul, layer sequencing, weather and traffic constraints add time.
- Concrete slab placement150m³ per productive day. B requires70,000×0.25=17,500m³, or116.7 productive slab days, excluding lean concrete, joints, inspections and curing.
- C requires11,515t asphalt(23.0 productive days) and5250m³ slab(35.0 productive days). Those activities may use different crews, so do not simply treat their sum as the critical path.
- Two-sided drains total20,000m. Four crews at50m per crew per productive day require100 productive workdays. Difficult outfalls and major cross-drains can extend the programme substantially.

For new concrete construction, DoR's current specification requires28-day traffic closure after slab placement and at least7 days between lean concrete and pavement slab. Wet hessian curing requirements and quality acceptance remain mandatory; a nominal strength milestone does not automatically waive the closure clause. A staged half-width programme needs safe traffic access, enough space, and engineer approval. Ordinary asphalt can be reopened after specified cooling and acceptance; it still needs weather/temperature and compaction controls.

Do not turn32.9 productive asphalt days into a promise to build10km in a month. Drainage, investigations, procurement, earthworks, base acceptance, traffic management and the monsoon calendar determine elapsed duration. The main plan should reserve several dry-season construction months plus preparation and procurement, and monitor at least two monsoons before broad scaling. Compare the **same corridor scope** when reporting construction durations.

## Reproduce and replace inputs

Run from the project folder:

```text
python models/pavement_model.py
node models/verify_model.mjs
```

Files:

- `models/pavement_model.py`: standard-library calculation, quantities, rates, maintenance, matrix and deterministic scenario grid.
- `models/results.json`: structured results, assumptions, source links, all cash flows, matrix sensitivities and productivity calculations.
- `models/pavement_model.js`: browser calculation for interactive parameter changes.
- `models/quantities_and_rates.csv`: auditable quantity/rate schedule with analyst low/high ranges.
- `models/cashflows.csv`: undiscounted and discounted annual rows for each option.
- `models/sensitivity.csv`:243-case cost sensitivity table.
- `models/selection_matrix.csv`: criteria, weights, scores and reasons.
- `models/verify_model.mjs`: arithmetic consistency checks between browser and Python.

First replace the route and condition inventory; then axle-load spectrum, growth, saturated subgrade strength and drainage hydraulics; then locally designed structural sections; then actual installed rates; then evidence-based service schedules and user disruption costs. Rerun the cost model and independent selection review before awarding a construction contract.
